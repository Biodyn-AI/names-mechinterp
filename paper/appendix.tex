\section*{S1 Study scope and data construction}

This study concerns explicit fictional registries, not the earlier country-fact experiments or four-world pilots. The new generator froze 32 development worlds and 64 planned confirmation worlds. Only development worlds received model inference. Files named REGISTRATION record local, hash-linked execution freezes, not public preregistration. The additional six-world diagnosis was designed after inspecting failed validation and is not independent confirmation.


Each world contains four permanent object IDs, four distinct values for each of color, shape and home, and four alias bindings. Two aliases denote the same object; another denotes a distractor, and a fourth provides context. Nameplates are labels, not permanent IDs. The eight selected alias words were apple, bat, anchor, date, cat, basket, fig and dog. Selection used token geometry in both model tokenizers, without model answers. Lexemes recur across worlds, but complete ordered object-to-value assignments do not.


Each world supplies 96 prompts: two equivalent-name orders, four Focus conditions, and twelve probes. These comprise nine factual questions over three aliases, two identity questions, and one registry-status question. Focus is absent, names either equivalent alias, or names an unrelated alias. Name orders exchange the equivalent words across binding-line locations without changing their referent. Across worlds, target-record and alias-line positions, object IDs, semantic name roles, and two wording formats are balanced; not every factor combination is fully crossed. Both models receive the same worlds: 3,072 prompts per model, not 64 independent worlds.


\section*{S2 Pinned models, execution and grading}

Models use their pinned tokenizer and chat rendering. Inference uses eager attention, batch size one, float32, disabled TensorFloat-32, one CPU thread, and native full-vocabulary output scores at one position. Answers are generated greedily. Whole decoded answers are normalized for case, whitespace and final punctuation; explanations are not silently cut off. Invalid or extra text remains an error. Causal generation permits eight new tokens and records end-of-sequence termination; none of the final diagnostic answers was truncated.

\section*{S3 Search sequence and the six repair cases}

The exploratory screen first replaced pre-field activations at the end-of-alias marker, before the requested field appeared. It searched 35 layer boundaries and then heads in two selected layers on eight development cases. A separate answer-position routing search used the same eight cases, with field-visible donors. Across all 36 layers and 32 heads, it selected two candidates using correct-versus-wrong record-attention contrasts. Selection used answer labels; the subsequent full four-record profile operation did not. The best candidate, zero-based layer 28, head 0, changed five of eight first-token choices. This is feasibility evidence, not complete-answer repair.


Validation fixed that head and used the six remaining eligible development worlds, disjoint from the eight routing cases but already behavior-screened. Eligibility required a correct donor across all three fields and a listed wrong-record recipient answer. There were eight erroneous and ten protected correct recipient field answers. The final decomposition reopened these exact six worlds, keeping the component and cases fixed.

\section*{S5 Nine fixed decomposition operators}

Record mass means summed attention probability over a printed object's record tokens. The profile divides each record's mass by total record attention; the within-record pattern divides each token's weight by its record mass. Four factor conditions retain recipient total record attention. Donor and recipient record value vectors must be bytewise identical. We compute head-output changes before the attention output projection, using float64 coefficients and float32 contractions. Each field-specific change is held fixed at the original answer-query position throughout decoding, not moved to newly generated positions.

\section*{S6 Frozen criteria, failures and statistical scope}

The original validation required four of eight repairs spanning at least three worlds, at most one damaged protected answer, and at least two more repairs than the frozen discovery-mean profile. Reverse and cyclic interventions each required nine of eighteen specific predicted-record answers across three worlds; cyclic success additionally required two target IDs. The primary profile repaired one error, in world 029. Reverse and cyclic tests each produced one specific hit. All gates failed; no untouched confirmation inference followed.


The final nine-condition diagnosis generated 162 answers. Seven new conditions each repaired zero of eight errors; replaying the old profile retained its one repair. Every condition preserved the ten protected answers. This rejects the tested simple repair, not every possible binding mechanism. Saved first-step scores show genuine effects: the old profile improved the correct-minus-fixed-old-wrong canonical-token margin by 6.00 on average, same-field profile by 4.05, and whole-head transfer by 4.21. These are descriptive score changes, not complete-answer accuracy or accepted-variant probability sums.


Behavioral intervals resample whole worlds 2,000 times, retaining repeated questions and conditions. They are descriptive development intervals, not confirmation or independent-question tests. Additional wrong-record intervals use retrospective seed 20261115. Predictive comparisons hold out whole-world folds; intervals conditional on saved predictions do not include refitting or model-selection uncertainty.


\section*{S7 Numerical audits and source anchors}

Natural head reconstruction and frozen-query drift were bounded by 0.00002; observed query drift was zero. Record profiles require total mass at least 0.0001 and per-record support at least 0.00000001. Full-head reconstruction, record/residual recombination and random-norm checks passed. Thirty-six natural forwards replayed; eighteen self and eighteen old-profile full-answer trajectories matched exactly. Saved tensors reproduced native scores, and independent literal grading agreed. The final run used 360 native forwards. No failed gate, component or dose was changed to obtain success.
